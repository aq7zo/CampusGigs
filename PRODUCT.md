# CampusGigs

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary users are undergraduate students aged 18–24 with five to twenty free hours scattered around a fixed class timetable. They have a marketable skill—such as writing, design, coding, tutoring, or administration—but limited freelance experience and little negotiating leverage.

Small businesses and solo founders are a secondary audience. They need affordable, well-scoped work completed in short, compatible windows, but the employer-side workflow remains secondary to the student experience.

## Product Purpose

CampusGigs helps students turn gaps in their timetable into freelance work. It matches gigs against declared availability and weekly capacity, while showing real demand-to-supply ratios before students invest time in a skill.

Success means that no recommended gig conflicts with a declared busy period, every skill exposes its demand signal, and a student can move from profile input to credible matches in under sixty seconds.

The intended journey is onboarding followed by the actual workspace. The current project is the workspace. A future onboarding surface will use a brand/marketing register, but it is outside the scope of the present foundations work.

## Positioning

CampusGigs treats student availability as a first-class credential and combines it with market demand to find work that genuinely fits.

## Brand Personality

Precise, practical, and credible. The voice is direct and grounded: it helps students make informed choices without overstating certainty or disguising sample data as guaranteed outcomes.

## Anti-references

CampusGigs should not resemble a generic Fiverr or Upwork clone, a childish student portal, or a futuristic AI dashboard. Avoid marketplace theatre, decorative “smart” effects, and visual tropes that weaken trust in the underlying scheduling and demand data.

## Design Principles

1. **Make availability useful.** Treat time constraints as meaningful matching data and a visible part of the student’s professional profile.
2. **Show the market truth.** Make demand, supply, rates, and compatibility legible before asking users to commit effort.
3. **Keep cause and effect immediate.** Profile edits should update insights and matches without a submission step or hidden processing.
4. **Earn trust through restraint.** Prefer familiar controls, clear language, and honest claims over decorative marketplace conventions.
5. **Build the foundation before the funnel.** Stabilize and review the actual workspace before designing the onboarding surface around it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. The workspace must support full keyboard use, readable contrast, status cues that do not rely on color alone, and reduced-motion preferences. Responsive behavior must preserve a complete, legible workflow at narrow mobile widths.
